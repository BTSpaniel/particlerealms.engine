# Surface Fields and Surface Lab

Surface fields add thin liquid films and finite reactions to the engine's existing material catalog, pine combustion, Matter inventories, and native Blast/PhysX stack. This guide explains the owners and APIs used by Surface Lab: water on pine, oil on copper, and hydrochloric acid on calcite, with four corner supports per panel. WebGPU transports both liquid phases and runs the existing native Flow gas simulation and volume renderer. A dedicated worker retains the CPU material and chemical owners so their work can proceed separately from presentation. Fired clay remains available for the receiving floor and existing callers.

## Run the playground

Start `python start_server.py` from the repository root. Open `http://127.0.0.1:9001/tests/playground/?demo=surface-lab` and select Surface Lab if the host does not apply the query automatically.

The default sequence ignites wood, ignites oil at 1.5 s, starts rain on pine at 2 s, doses calcite with acid at 3 s, and floods the pine at 4 s. It oils and ignites every attached copper-panel rope once at 4.5 s and doses every attached calcite-panel steel wire once with acid at 5.5 s. Automatic rain stops at 8 simulation seconds, and the simulation continues running. Rebuild sample replays with fresh stock. Initial water is a small 0.08 mm centre film; water brushes deposit 0.8 mm/s. The acid brush covers a 1.2 m radius with a finite 1.35 s equivalent dose of the existing 30% solution. Flood pine applies a finite 20 s equivalent water dose over a 1.7 m brush footprint. Both use the existing weighted brush law, so their total volumes depend on the intersected cells.

The support controls supply explicit finite doses. **Burn oil ropes** puts 2 g of oil on each of six segments of every attached copper-panel fiber rope, then heats each rope's bottom segment to ignite it: 12 g per rope, or 48 g for four intact ropes. **Acid on steel wires** applies 100 g of 30% HCl solution to each attached wire's bottom segment: four intact wires receive 120 g HCl and 280 g carrier water. Each dose contains enough finite reactant to consume that link's iron and remove its tensile capacity; the initial carbon residue remains in the material owner. These actions expose all remaining load paths to material damage so the copper and surviving calcite can fall. They do not replenish spent doses or schedule cuts. Wet ropes adds 5 g of water per segment, or 30 g per fiber rope. Cut corner severs one pine-panel support through the native bridge. Quenched pine remains held while its own supports are intact. These controls expose the support owner directly; a panel brush reaches a support only through admitted contact at its authored corner.

Each support action reports its affected corner or rope count beside the controls. Attached counts distinguish a severed corner from a board still held by its other supports. Paused edits retain their finite dose without advancing time; **Resume reactions** starts fire, corrosion and native motion. Burn, acid and cut buttons become unavailable when no matching attached support remains, and rebuilding restores them. Wetting still reaches retained cut fiber ends. (Sources: `tests/playground/src/demos/surfaceLab/index.js` and `ui.js`.)

Support appearance follows retained segment inventories. Water darkens fiber and smooths its highlight; oil uses the existing darker glossy material profile. HCl applies the lab's diagnostic green tint to a coated wire, fades with dilution and is obscured by a separate oil film. These appearance updates preserve physical geometry, strength and material state. Acid remains chemically clear in reality; this tint does not imply light emission or new reaction kinetics. (Source: `engine/render/surfaceFields/SurfaceFieldSupportRenderer.js`.)

Left-drag paints water, oil, acid, ignition, heat, finite pine coating, or a wipe. Right-drag orbits the camera. Rain uses millimetres per hour; brush dimensions and material transfers use SI units. Only Pause, Space, or Single step pauses the simulation for inspection. A runtime failure displays `Stopped: error` and requires a rebuilt sample or a valid saved sample before further stepping. The state views share the same retained owners. The clock reports render CPU time separately from the completed material and gas step's wall time.

Take apart samples breaks the panels' remaining native Blast contacts without changing their chemical mass. PhysX moves the surviving leaves, with corner joints retaining only their attached fragments. Rebuild sample supplies fresh stock and creates new owners; it does not regrow dissolved matter or heal damaged Blast bonds.

Source: `tests/playground/src/demos/surfaceLab/index.js` and `ui.js`.

Existing physics playground Flow rendering helpers now live in `engine/render/flow/index.js`; their original playground imports remain compatible wrappers. `EngineBootstrap.js` also exports `SurfaceFields`, `SurfaceFieldRendering`, `FlowRendering`, and `WaterThermodynamics` namespaces.

The production demo's material worker prepares rain-adjusted solvent water and pressure heads. Its serial transport callback sends copied arrays to the main thread's borrowed-device WebGPU runtime and receives bounded directed fluxes. The Matter owner moves each donor's water, dissolved HCl, calcium chloride and liquid heat in proportion to those fluxes. Only after that transfer commits does the world prepare oil's support from the updated aqueous layer and request the second GPU pass, with the existing 0.2 mobility multiplier. The two requests cannot run in parallel against stale layer heights.

`addWaters(entries)` batches rain admission, while `takeAqueousWaters(entries, { kind })` batches absorption or evaporation with each cell's retained phase availability and heat. The worker then runs the existing material and chemical owners. The main thread receives completed field arrays, updates native structural masses and contacts, and presents them. Measured sources accumulate for the native Flow cadence on the borrowed GPU device. Pine combustion, phase energies, Matter inventories and conservation remain CPU authority at their retained precision; GPU kernels compute the bounded Float32 transport field. This is a hybrid simulation, not a GPU rewrite of the combustion and chemistry laws.

Rain, absorption, evaporation and lateral liquid transport use the existing Matter operator's packed transfer form internally. It snapshots typed species, source/target indices and Float64 quantity rows before validation. This removes intermediate transfer objects while keeping public input checks, donor limits, immutable evidence and conservation checks. Water admission and export still return the same per-cell plans and results.

Source: `engine/sim/surfaceFields/SurfaceFieldWorld.js`, `SurfaceFieldGpu.js`, `SurfaceFieldWorker.js`, `SurfaceFieldWorkerClient.js`, and `tests/playground/src/demos/surfaceLab/index.js`.

Matter receipts reuse validated, deeply immutable states through `snapshotMatterState()` and `snapshotMatterStates()`. A private provenance set recognizes only states validated and frozen by the contract module. `replaceMatterConserved()` shares their unchanged metadata while copying and validating each new conserved inventory, revision and derived-cache change. Raw inputs still receive detached copies and full schema validation; `createMatterState()` continues to copy its input. This avoids repeated state copies during liquid transfers without changing their arithmetic or validation rules.

`replaceMatterConservedPacked()` accepts packed species and Float64 dynamics while retaining the full replacement's revision, derived-state and validation rules. `snapshotMatterNumericArray()` copies admitted typed numeric arrays and rejects shared backing memory, including buffers from another realm. These paths reduce temporary objects inside the existing CPU authority. Source: `engine/matter/contracts/MatterContracts.js`.

The surface owner also reuses cell summaries within one completed step. Field projection, emissions and returned totals consume the same post-reaction wood summaries; an explicit later `stats()` call recomputes current totals. Worker presentation reads carbonate directly from its validated retained inventory. Neither optimization caches a material result across an edit or a subsequent step.

Surface transfers explicitly request the Matter ledger's `binary64-summary-v1` energy-rounding evidence. Independent large energy totals can differ by one representable floating-point step even when the underlying transfer conserves energy. The receipt retains that raw error and records an energy-only allowance derived from one ULP of each before, after, and external-energy summary. Assertion recomputes the allowance. The default Matter ledger remains at its absolute tolerance; species mass, momentum and charge never use the energy allowance. No phase energy is changed to force rounded totals to agree.

Copper and fired clay contain no combustible substrate. The Pine coating brush deposits actual finite pine mass on either material. Fired clay currently lacks catalog density, heat capacity, and conductivity: its substrate heat response remains unavailable and the UI reports the missing properties. Deposited pine still uses the existing pine model.

## Retained topology and fields

`createSurfaceFieldTopology()` builds planar domains with world-space areas and directed runoff edges. Rotated storage charts permute cell addresses without changing world-space neighbors or transport. Panel runoff enters an authored receiving floor. The static rain mask shelters floor cells covered by the authored panels; native motion additionally scales panel rain by visible projected area. The mask does not recompute moving shadow occlusion. This is an authored surface domain adapter, not automatic topology extraction from arbitrary meshes.

Each cell stores eight Float32 presentation channels:

| Index | Meaning | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Unabsorbed solvent-water equivalent depth, including acid carrier water | m |
| 1 | Remaining virgin pine coating | kg/m² |
| 2 | Surface temperature | K |
| 3 | Retained substrate damage | fraction |
| 4 | Optical char/ash residue coverage | fraction |
| 5 | Native pine moisture | kg/m² |
| 6 | Remaining virgin pine substrate | kg/m² |
| 7 | Steam/mist source rate | kg/m²/s |

Native layered masses and energy remain retained separately at their original precision. Changing render quality changes presentation detail; it does not resample or replace material owners.

`world.auxiliary` provides eight additional Float32 presentation channels:

| Index | Meaning | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Retained oil depth | m |
| 1 | Total retained aqueous solution depth, including solvent and dissolved species | m |
| 2 | Removed mineral depth | m |
| 3 | Carbonate reaction rate | kg/m²/s |
| 4 | Oil burn rate | kg/m²/s |
| 5 | CO₂ source rate | kg/m²/s |
| 6 | Remaining mass-equivalent pine solid depth | m |
| 7 | Initial pine solid depth | m |

Pine depth uses the retained virgin, char and ash masses divided by the initial dry mass, at the original dry pine density. Moisture does not restore consumed geometry. The renderer lowers the surface as native dry solid mass leaves and retains char and ash as solid geometry. This compacted depth is a geometric closure; char expansion and detached residue motion remain outside the model. `surfaceFieldSolidDepths()` supplies the same recession to demo picking. Dissolved calcite becomes solution and gas rather than invented solid debris.

`world.flowSources` provides four Float32 source channels per storage cell: wood gas heat in W/m², oil gas heat in W/m², oil product rate in kg/m²/s, and steam rate in kg/m²/s. `world.emissions` retains the measured native pine volatile and steam rates. Material and chemical owners retain their original mass and energy ledgers; these arrays expose their observations.

`world.structuralState` is a separate Float64 array with four entries per storage cell: reference solid mass in kg, current retained substrate mass in kg, surviving strength fraction, and additional carried mass in kg. For native pine, the reference is initial dry mass and retained mass includes its current moisture. The fourth entry includes aqueous/oil films and any deposited pine coating. The native bridge updates actor masses and remaining Blast contact areas from this completed material frame. Strength and mass have separate meanings: char and ash still weigh something after their virgin-fiber load capacity is gone.

`world.liquidProperties` provides four Float32 presentation channels per cell: HCl mass fraction in the aqueous phase, aqueous temperature in K, total aqueous depth in m, and oil coverage fraction. Oil mass never counts as aqueous diluent. The acid diagnostic tint follows retained HCl concentration; spent calcium-chloride solution retains its water layer without a permanent green acid tint.

The renderer accepts `upload(fields, { emissions, auxiliary, poses, liquidProperties, runoff })`. Aqueous depth is already complete in auxiliary channel 1; adding field channel 0 to it would count the solvent twice. Oil is rendered above that layer. Optional native poses contain eight floats per storage cell: centre XYZ, binary visibility, and quaternion XYZW. Appearance and sources follow the native leaves. The demo uses physical recession depth consistently for rendering, picking and native gas sources. Its 0.2 mm calcite sample can therefore settle on the floor without burying surviving material. The engine retains optional display magnification for other callers; a hole still requires the retained physical mineral to be exhausted. Pine recession uses its actual mass-equivalent depth.

Surface Lab supplies the existing Engine `mat4PerspectiveReverseZ` to the playground camera for each rendered frame and opts into `reverseZ: true` on the surface and support renderers. Their `depth32float` attachment clears to zero and compares greater depth values. This preserves thin landed fragments at the whole-lab viewing distance without moving their physical surfaces. Flow receives the same explicit convention for background detection, clipping and cached depth witnesses; the convention is part of its camera cache key. Shared renderer defaults and rope shadow maps retain forward depth. The camera projection choice is local to the call, so it does not change another demo's camera. (Sources: `SurfaceFieldRenderer.js`, `SurfaceFieldSupportRenderer.js`, `flowPresentation.js`, `flowVolume.js` and `tests/playground/src/core/context.js`.)

Surface solid fragments compute depth from each transformed physical face plane. `SurfaceFieldRenderer` reuses the existing `mat4Determinant` and `mat4Inverse` helpers to supply the inverse of the same packed camera matrix used for rendering; `SurfaceFieldShaders` evaluates each face plane at the covered pixel. This avoids depth errors from screen-space triangle rounding without moving vertices, inflating retained thickness or changing native poses, material mass or picking. A degenerate camera projection retains raster depth. Sources: `engine/render/surfaceFields/SurfaceFieldRenderer.js`, `SurfaceFieldShaders.js`, `engine/core/math/EngineMath.js` and `MathMat.js`.

Acid effervescence uses the retained carbonate reaction rate, aqueous depth and oil coverage. The shared phase bubble kernel produces moving pale interfaces and bounded normal agitation. Exhausted acid, absent aqueous liquid, stopped carbonate reaction or a fully covering oil film suppress this effect. Total CO₂ alone cannot identify acid attack because it also includes oil exhaust. The green tint remains a concentration diagnostic rather than HCl's actual colour.

Source: `engine/sim/surfaceFields/SurfaceFieldWorld.js` and `engine/render/surfaceFields/SurfaceFieldShaders.js`.

## Floods and airborne liquid

Pass `runoff: true` to `createSurfaceFieldWorld()` or `createSurfaceFieldWorker()` to enable the panel-to-air-to-floor handoff used by Surface Lab. Existing callers retain their direct surface routing by default. Directed GPU flux remains a validated proposal; the chemical owner redirects eligible panel-edge transfers into finite parcels. The receiving cell gets no mass before impact. Aqueous parcels carry water, HCl, calcium chloride and sensible heat together; oil parcels retain their separate phase. Airborne inventories contribute to mass, heat and water residuals.

`SurfaceFieldRunoff` allows at most 2,048 active parcels. Releases below 10⁻⁹ m³ stay on the donor surface until enough liquid accumulates. A full parcel budget or unsupported landing also retains donor liquid. Parcels do not expire or discard mass. Their trajectory uses an authored 0.25 m/s edge release and 9.81 m/s² gravity, with the first intersected authored receiving plane owning deposition. With a native motion frame, the origin follows the transformed edge and launch velocity includes the material point's linear and angular motion. Fractured internal edges become release edges; disconnected pieces cannot transfer liquid through their old neighbor link. This is a ballistic handoff for the reduced surface-film model; it does not run the SPH demo's full pressure solver, interparticle collisions or an energy-conserving momentum solve.

`world.runoff` and the worker facade expose a read-only Float32 presentation carrier with 16 floats per parcel: origin XYZ and birth time, velocity XYZ and impact time, equivalent sphere radius, phase (0 aqueous, 1 oil), HCl fraction and temperature, then destination XYZ and volume. Radius derives from the retained phase volume. The renderer evaluates physical-time trajectories on the GPU, applies a volume-preserving ellipsoid shape, and writes the true intersected surface depth. It shares the analytic ellipsoid kernel extracted from the current SPH demo, plus existing Fresnel, refraction, absorption and fire lighting. It adds one bounded draw to the existing liquid pass. Empty uploads clear the visible parcel count.

The bounded presentation clock can place a drop at its impact position before the next 50 ms material tick commits deposition. Its position then stays clamped at impact; its retained mass remains airborne until that tick. Pause freezes this clock. Whole lab uses a lower viewing angle to show the fall below the panel, and Water close-up frames the pine edge and receiving puddles.

`runoffCount`, `airborneWaterKg`, `airborneOilKg`, `airborneHClKg`, `airborneSaltKg`, `airborneEnergyJ`, `runoffBlockedVolumeM3` and `runoffDepositedCount` report the retained handoff. Source: `SurfaceFieldRunoff.js`, `SurfaceFieldChemistry.js`, `SurfaceFieldWorker.js`, `SurfaceFieldRenderer.js` and `SurfaceFieldRunoffShaders.js`. The current SPH renderer also imports the shared `particle_ellipsoid.js` kernel through the bundle-aware module loader.

## Native Flow fire presentation

`createSurfaceFieldFlow()` reuses `createFlowSolver()` and the existing sparse volume pipeline from the Blast and Flow playground. Native Flow owns gas advection, pressure, buoyancy, cooling and sparse GPU fields. `FlowSnapshot` copies completed native density and addressing data before the next step. `FlowPresentation` ray marches that sparse field with the existing blackbody, Beer-Lambert, phase and lighting helpers. Fire renders as a gas volume rather than flame or smoke sprites.

`buildSurfaceFieldFlowScene()` projects measured pine volatile rates, oil burn rates, gas heat, oil products and steam into ordinary native box emitters. Emitters follow the receded material surface and native leaf poses. `SURFACE_FLOW_PROJECTION` declares the normalized temperature, burn and smoke mapping. Those concentrations are presentation values, not Kelvin, kilograms, or a measured soot inventory. Native fuel and its coupling rate remain zero, so the existing material reactions do not combust a second time. Native combustion mode enables thermal buoyancy and cooling; the pinned gas reaction branch requires positive fuel.

Native source boxes retain a 0.025 m horizontal reconstruction margin. This closes point-sampling gaps between adjacent material tiles. Their measured rates, centres, finite inventories and retained geometry stay unchanged; the margin belongs to the derived gas presentation.

The current authored display mapping bounds normalized burn at 0.08 and temperature at 0.85. Pine volatile and oil burn references are 0.012 and 0.06 kg/m²/s, and the heat reference is 100 kW/m². The bounded responses fit the existing shared fire radiance curve; they do not cap measured source rates or change finite material reactions. Signed heat observations stay in the source integrals. The temperature target uses the positive part of the averaged heat rather than claiming exact native heat delivery.

When measured sources are active, the scene applies the Blast and Flow pattern of two oblique air inlets and a broad draft around each source footprint. Coherent engine Perlin gusts vary these velocities by at most 0.75 m/s per axis, with a smaller 0.12 m/s vertical variation on the side inlets. These ordinary native box emitters couple only velocity. Their heat, burn, smoke, fuel and divergence coupling rates are zero. The broad inlet uses at most `[1.1, 0.3, 0.7]` m half extents and an authored mean 2.4 m/s upward velocity target with coupling 4/s. `applyPostPressure: false` lets native pressure resolve the inlet velocities; native advection and vorticity produce the moving plume. The inlets add no reaction or material inventory. A scene with no measured sources has no inlets.

The adapter uses 0.05 m density cells, native vorticity 8, and a maximum of 256 sparse blocks. The default native velocity cells are twice the density-cell width, matching [NVIDIA's Flow grid settings](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/flow/latest/settings.html). The installed scene supports up to 1024 active emitters. Measured material sources receive those slots first. If support sources need space in a full table, the projection merges existing optical boxes into a containing box with volume-weighted optical values. `aggregatedOpticalBoxes` reports this spatial coarsening; the separate measured SI source totals remain complete. Optional air inlets use the remaining slots, and `airflowSkippedCount` / `airflowSkippedReason` report any omitted inlets.

The shared fire shader combines the existing blackbody colour lookup with `blackbodySpectralRatio()` at 650 nm, normalized to the authored 2600 K display reference. Its Planck response uses the [NIST second radiation constant](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Results?search_for=second+radiation), approximately 0.01438776877 m·K. This single-wavelength brightness proxy suppresses the luminous halo of cooling gas; it is not a calibrated full-spectrum radiation model. Native temperature still maps to an authored 1000–2600 K colour range. Native burn and heat gate emission, and the engine's ACES tone mapping compresses the result. Both Surface Lab and Blast and Flow use these shared optical helpers.

Surface Lab additionally opts into `fireDetail` in the volume shader. Existing engine `noise3d()` attenuates temperature and burn only inside already hot, burning native samples. It adds moving gaps and fine flame structure without increasing either channel or changing fuel, smoke, source inventories or native grid settings. The detail clock uses completed native simulation time, so a held frame is stable while paused. Blast and Flow's default remains unchanged. The cheaper periodic turbulence trial produced visible parallel flame bands and was rejected after visual review. The retained value-noise kernel costs more per ray but preserves the irregular detail. Shader-only trials are recorded under `artifacts/surface-fields-flood-fire/fire-trials/`; they do not establish end-to-end performance.

Render detail defaults to Auto, using the same measured presentation governor as Blast and Flow. Low, Medium, High and Full select the existing performance, balanced, high and full tiers. Numeric callers retain 1/2/4 for Low/Medium/High; Auto is 0 and Full is 5. These choices leave native gas cell size and material ownership unchanged. The ray marcher samples twice per native density cell. Full renders each output pixel directly; paused lower-resolution frames refine to full resolution without advancing simulation.

The surface renderer supplies completed GPU timestamp samples when supported, CPU encoding time, display cadence and queue completion observations to the shared governor. Timing prioritizes changed native or surface revisions, so sampling cached frames cannot systematically miss the volume refresh. Other frames are sampled every eight submissions when a readback slot is available. `beginPresentation()` observes the previous frame before encoding another one; a bounded submission queue can defer drawing while the simulation continues. Call `didSubmit()` after submitting the renderer's command encoder. Paused Flow refinement uses the same shared presentation path and preserves the material clock.

`FlowPresentation.create(device, format, source, { targetFPS })` accepts a presentation target and defaults to 60 FPS for existing callers, including Blast and Flow. Surface Lab requests 120 FPS, an 8.33 ms frame budget. Auto adjusts presentation quality against that target; this setting does not establish an achieved frame rate. The main-thread renderer can draw the last completed material and gas snapshots while the worker or native gas step is pending. The demo caches completed poses and invalidates picking when the pointer, camera, display geometry or retained presentation changes. Its FPS header counts submitted presentations rather than merely counting animation callbacks.

`FlowComputeScheduler` is an optional engine adapter used by Surface Lab. It gives the existing native solver a borrowed-device facade and wraps each native step with `scheduler.run(() => solver.step(dt))`. Commands encoded synchronously by that callback are partitioned after complete compute passes, preserving their original order and interleaved copies. The existing `GpuFrameBudgetBroker` admits them with a 4 ms compute budget and at most two submissions awaiting completion. GPU timestamps learn per-pass admission costs when supported. Passes without a timestamp or provisional startup estimate, and unclassified copy tails, use a conservative 4 ms estimate. Native steps, sources, field resolution and shader arithmetic remain unchanged. Individual passes are indivisible, so an oversized pass can still exceed the presentation budget. Scheduling can exchange simulation throughput for presentation headroom; it does not establish 120 FPS.

Consecutive passes with timestamp or provisional startup estimates are submitted together when their summed estimate fits the same budget, reducing queue calls and completion fences. Measured costs retain a 25% safety margin and a 0.001 ms floor; the broker accounts for submission overhead once per batch. Passes without either estimate, unclassified copy tails and oversized passes remain separate. Native queries outside the capture retain immediate submission, including `submit()` followed by `mapAsync()`. The facade's completion promise waits for deferred submissions and the actual GPU fence before native readback or resource retirement. Await native solver disposal before `scheduler.dispose()`; both leave the real device owned by the caller. `flow.stats.computeScheduling` reports admission, pass timings, failures and timestamp buffer ownership. Normal Blast and Flow callers retain their existing submission path unless they explicitly use this adapter.

Surface Lab opts into `new FlowComputeScheduler(device, { texturePool: true, computeBudgetMs: 2, maxInFlightSubmissions: 2, readbackTailBudget: { maxCopyBytes: 2048, maxTimingBytes: 1024, estimatedGpuMs: .25 } })`. The two-submission bound avoids a completion fence between every admitted batch while the 2 ms budget leaves headroom for presentation and liquid readbacks. These are admission estimates, not hard GPU execution limits. Other callers retain the scheduler's existing defaults. The installed native host does not pool atlases larger than 32 MiB; its 256-block density atlas is about 49 MiB. The adapter retains compatible retired 3D float textures and explicitly zeros every mip before the next native use, through the same ordered GPU command path. A lease is reusable only after the preceding native run and GPU work finish. The default limit is 192 MiB of idle texture storage, with at most four idle textures per exact descriptor. `texturePool` can instead supply `maxIdleBytes`, `minTextureBytes` and `maxTexturesPerDescriptor`. This option defaults to disabled for other scheduler callers.

`readbackTailBudget` defaults to disabled for other callers. An explicit configuration can admit a small native readback tail alongside preceding measured passes when their total estimate fits the compute budget. Surface Lab configures a 0.25 ms estimate for one aligned buffer copy of at most 2048 bytes into a distinct `MAP_READ | COPY_DST` buffer, followed by the scheduler's successful timestamp resolve and copy of at most 1024 bytes. A preceding native compute pass is required. Additional copies, clears, texture operations, caller-owned queries, internal pool-clear passes, unavailable profiler results and oversized work retain conservative admission. All original command buffers, copies, query buffers and physical completion fences remain intact.

The configured estimate comes from a diagnostic capture of 223 native tails on the measured GPU: the largest combined copy/profiler interval was 0.131072 ms. Adding the observed 0.065536 ms timer quantum and a 25% margin gives 0.24576 ms, rounded to 0.25 ms. This is admission guidance, not a portable execution-time limit. Quantized zero samples do not establish zero cost. `flow.stats.computeScheduling` exposes `readbackTailBudget`, `readbackTailEligibleBuffers`, `readbackTailSubmittedBuffers` and cumulative `readbackTailEstimatedGpuMs`; the last counter reports configured admission estimates rather than measured GPU execution time.

The lease pool is scoped to the installed native host's single-owner lifecycle: release each lease once and discard its views before reacquiring that allocation. It returns real `GPUTexture` objects, preserves fresh-texture zero contents, and does not alter native shader arithmetic or field resolution. `stats.texturePool` distinguishes physical allocations and reuses from the native host's logical allocation counter. Native `allocatedBytes` accounts for active leases; scheduler `bytes` adds only idle and retiring texture storage plus its own timestamp buffers. Disposing the native solver before the scheduler releases the retained allocations.

`FlowSnapshot` retains compatible density textures, sparse tables and lighting textures independently. Each completed native frame still copies fresh field contents. Resource generation changes only when a GPU resource is replaced, allowing bounds, shadow and stencil bind groups to be reused safely while their inputs retain the same identities.

Surface Lab schedules outer material and liquid updates at 20 Hz, matching the existing 0.05 s native gas interval. `WoodCombustion` retains its adaptive thermal and reaction substeps inside each outer update. The completed material clock advances only after actual owner work; presentation refresh does not accelerate that clock. Changing the outer interval from 1/60 s changes the split thermal and transport trajectory, so this is not a claim of thermal identity with the previous cadence. Exact checkpoint continuation is checked at the same cadence.

`flow.step()` integrates every admitted source interval into Float64 accumulators. Once 0.05 simulation seconds accumulate, it projects the time-weighted average rates using the current surface geometry and awaits a native gas step. Completed projection targets and pending input integrals remain visible in `stats.sourceProjectionIntegrals`; they describe source scheduling, not native SI deposition receipts. A failed native step blocks further source admission until the instance is replaced.

The installed native addon exposes ordinary scene emitters, not the newer scalar-source deposition receipt API. Surface Lab therefore makes no SI gas deposition or native gas conservation claim. Exact mass and energy remain in the material and chemical owners. Native gas visualization restarts when loading an exact material checkpoint or rebuilding fresh stock. Its sparse atlas is not part of the exact material and motion checkpoint.

Await `flow.step(dt, { fields, emissions, auxiliary, flowSources, poses })` before another Flow step or a field query. Calls below the native cadence retain the sources without launching a gas step. Drawing uses the last completed snapshot while a step is pending. ## Aqueous mixtures, oil and damp pine

Water, HCl and dissolved calcium chloride share one retained aqueous inventory per cell. Adding water conserves HCl while lowering `hclMassFraction`. For example, 20 g of the authored 30% acid dose contains 6 g HCl and 14 g water. Adding 40 g water gives 60 g solution at 10% HCl before reaction. The reduced calcite reaction rate multiplies its authored maximum by `clamp(hclMassFraction / 0.30, 0, 1)` and still stops at the finite reactant limit. This is a concentration response for the demonstration, not a calibrated electrolyte reaction law.

`transportPhase('aqueous', fluxM3)` uses solvent-water equivalent volume to transfer donor composition and energy together. `transferMatterInventories()` validates the complete batch against the original donor inventories, then builds one replacement per affected region and a shared conservation receipt. Incoming liquid cannot fund another outgoing edge during that same transfer. Exterior runoff, wiping, evaporation and water absorbed into pine retain separate owners and ledgers. The world rejects malformed or inconsistent GPU fluxes before admitting rain or modifying native owners. Pressure heads include dissolved-species volume, overlying oil mass and the physical solid recession.

Oil has its own retained mass and sensible heat. It remains above the aqueous phase, exchanges finite heat with water and moves over the aqueous support with a 0.2 mobility multiplier. Adding water or acid does not dilute oil into an aqueous solution. The reduced model does not solve emulsification, droplet breakup, surfactants, oil chemistry with acid, or burning-oil splash.

Water carried by acid can wet native pine. `takeAqueousWater(..., { kind: 'absorption' })` removes only available solvent and transfers its temperature and sensible heat through `WoodCombustion.applySurfaceTransfer()`. HCl and dissolved salt remain in the surface inventory, so selective absorption concentrates the remaining solution. Native moisture and thermal drying continue to use the established layered wood owner. Surface heat admission preserves the requested direction and magnitude even when a reaction reduces the layer's heat capacity. Checkpoints validate derived native temperatures against retained enthalpy; the 5000 K limit remains an authored input limit. Humidity-driven equilibrium moisture and acid damage to cellulose are outside this model.

Liquid stores and transports heat instead of treating free water as an infinite ambient heat sink. Aqueous and oil energies are retained separately; deriving a tiny water film's heat by subtracting a large oil energy would lose its temperature precision. Their sum supplies the conserved Matter energy. The shared water closure accounts for sensible heat, fusion and vaporization at atmospheric pressure. The Matter owner applies an explicit energy-reference offset so its nonnegative internal energy remains compatible with the shared ice-at-273.15 K reference. Boiling exports water and its paid enthalpy while retaining dissolved species. Lateral transport treats the aqueous inventory as a homogeneous film; freezing adhesion and ice or slush mechanics are not solved. Constant solute heat capacity and partial volume are declared closures. Neutralization uses an explicitly recorded isothermal thermal closure; the model does not predict measured heat of acid dilution or reaction, electrolyte activity, solubility limits, HCl volatility, or boiling-point elevation.

Complete phase depletion transfers the phase's retained energy, so an empty phase cannot leave orphaned heat. Oil/water exchange bounds both donor energy and receiver capacity in the requested direction. A donor's floating-point excess above a temperature cap cannot be forced into a trace water film. Liquid transport derives exported and retained heat from their actual phase masses, then adds incoming heat separately. The shared Matter batch likewise retains the donor remainder separately from incoming species, preserving a trace arrival when the original inventory drains. These changes keep the existing strict mass and energy checks; they do not discard trace water or relax thermal validation.

## Researched reaction profiles

Water suppression follows the existing pine heat and moisture paths. Fuel cooling and wetting are documented water suppression mechanisms in [NIST's fire fighting study](https://www.nist.gov/publications/fire-fighting-properties-nistir-6191). Rain is localized to the pine panel; it is not routed over the oil fire.

The reduced oil profile uses Fuel Oil #2 density 879 kg/m³, autoignition 529.8167 K, regression 4 mm/min, and heat of combustion 45.217 MJ/kg from [NOAA/USCG's fuel oil record](https://cameochemicals.noaa.gov/chris/OTW.pdf). Commercial oil is a mixture. `C12H26` is explicitly a composition proxy for conserved element bookkeeping, not a claim about the exact mixture. Its constant heat capacity, surface heat feedback and contact transfer are declared closures. Cold oil does not burn; ignition admits heat explicitly; burning stops when the finite inventory is empty.

Acid consumes finite HCl and calcite through `CaCO3 + 2 HCl → CaCl2(aq) + CO2 + H2O`. Dissolved salt and water remain in the owner, and CO₂ is exported to its retained gas inventory. [USGS describes carbonate stone dissolution in acid](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/stones/acid-rain.html); this is why the sample uses calcite rather than generic fired clay. Calcite density is 2710 kg/m³ from the [NIST SRM 915c report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-0dd1458c29aace6b60ecba3311f8a195/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-0dd1458c29aace6b60ecba3311f8a195.pdf). The 30% solution density and 0.12 kg/m²/s accelerated dissolution rate are declared demonstration closures. They do not assert universal stone attack kinetics. The engine's green acid appearance is a diagnostic tint; aqueous HCl is clear.

## Suspended panels and finite support materials

Surface Lab suspends all three panels from overhead rods through four native corner distance joints per panel. Pine and copper use 4 mm-radius fiber cords; calcite uses 1.5 mm-radius carbon-steel wires. The authored tensile inputs are 60 MPa for fiber and 180 MPa for steel, giving initial material limits of about 3016 N and 1272 N per support. These are demonstration parameters, not certified rope or wire ratings. The bridge also scales the joint limit by the remaining corner material strength. PhysX redistributes load and moves released actors; Blast owns the connected solid islands.

`SurfaceFieldSupportGeometry` supplies seven nodes for each six-segment support through the existing particle-chain solver. Its node order runs from panel attachment to overhead anchor. Native joints carry the panel forces; this chain provides sag and detached-tip geometry without adding a second rigid-body force owner. A cut marks a segment irreversibly, retains its mass, and leaves the lower remnant attached while its corner body exists. Its equivalent load at that corner contributes to the panel's native mass. This is not a fully coupled cable continuum, calibrated panel stress solver, or two-way fluid momentum solver.

`SurfaceFieldSupports` owns each segment's material. The `hemp-pine-proxy` profile uses hemp-like appearance but explicitly reuses `WoodCombustion` with the established FDS pine density, heat, moisture, char and pyrolysis laws. It does not silently claim calibrated hemp kinetics. The material's remaining virgin-fiber fraction sets strength; char and ash retain mass without virgin-fiber tensile capacity. Fully spent segments reuse the existing energy-preserving ash surface remesh. [Experimental hemp fire research](https://4spepublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/vnl.21978) supports treating hemp as combustible; it does not validate this rope approximation.

Finite oil and aqueous films remain separate on each segment. Contact at a surviving panel corner transfers admitted fractions of the panel's actual composition and heat to segment 0. Opposite signed mass, water and heat ledger entries connect the two inventories. Nearby intact fiber segments redistribute finite films through a bounded wicking approximation. Absorption moves only solvent water into fiber; HCl and salts remain outside. Water then warms and evaporates through the existing pine thermodynamics. Humidity equilibration and acid degradation of cellulose remain outside this support model.

Fire spreads through native solid conduction and a declared local heat-coupling approximation. Up to 8% upward or 2% downward of an adjacent segment's finite oil-gas heat returns to nearby material, with distance attenuation. Accepted heat is removed from the retained gas owner; the exported optical gas source reports the remaining heat. The receiving film and fiber must actually heat and react. No timer marks neighboring segments as burning. Cutting the solid path removes its conduction and wicking link; spatial heat transfer can still cross a nearby gap. These local heat fractions are authored parameters, not samples of the native Flow field.

The steel profile uses 99% Fe and 1% C bookkeeping composition, density 7850 kg/m³ and constant heat capacity 500 J/(kg·K). Its finite reaction is:

```text
Fe + 2 HCl → FeCl2(aq) + H2(g)
```

The iron and acid limit the reaction. FeCl2 stays in solution, hydrogen stays in the retained gas inventory, and the initial carbon remains as solid residue. Bare steel supplies no oil or fiber fuel, and produced hydrogen is not automatically burned. Water dilutes HCl and reduces the authored rate. The 0.4 kg/(m²·s) maximum is an accelerated demonstration closure, not measured corrosion kinetics. The reaction uses the existing constant solute heat/volume and explicit isothermal energy closure; it does not predict corrosion heat, oxide passivation, cracking or hydrogen embrittlement. The base-steel reaction is described in [Alcaraz et al.'s carbon-steel pickling study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8432721/).

Rope chemistry is material-specific. For example, [Marlow's rope-access chemical resistance table](https://marlowropes.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Rope-Access-Brochure.pdf) reports different residual strengths for nylon, polyester, polypropylene, aramid and HMPE under its stated acid exposure. Those measurements do not supply a universal rope dissolution law and are not substituted for the current fiber approximation.

Support rows feed the same native Flow projection as panels. Each row supplies its completed endpoints, volatile rate, oil burn/product rates, steam, and separate wood/oil gas heat. Source integration retains these SI observations before mapping them to bounded native optical emitters. H2 remains an inventory quantity, not a new native fuel source. The support shader renders material strands; fire continues to use the gas volume renderer.

## Use the APIs

```javascript
import {
    createSurfaceFieldTopology, loadSurfaceFieldMaterials,
    createSurfaceFieldWorld, createSurfaceFieldGpuRuntime,
    encodeSurfaceFieldCheckpoint, decodeSurfaceFieldCheckpoint,
} from '/engine/sim/surfaceFields/index.js';

const topology = createSurfaceFieldTopology({ n: 16, seams: true });
const materials = await loadSurfaceFieldMaterials();
const world = createSurfaceFieldWorld({ topology, materials, runoff: true });
const liquids = await createSurfaceFieldGpuRuntime({ device, topology });
const dt = 1 / 60, options = { rain: 30, tiltX: 0.05 };
world.applyBrush({ domain: 0, x: -2.75, z: 0, radius: 0.5,
    tool: 'water', strength: 1, duration: dt });
await world.stepAsync(dt, options, request => liquids.transport(request));

const checkpoint = encodeSurfaceFieldCheckpoint(world.snapshot());
world.restore(decodeSurfaceFieldCheckpoint(checkpoint.bytes));
liquids.dispose();
world.dispose();
```

`world.step(dt, options)` remains the synchronous CPU reference. `world.stepAsync(dt, options, transport)` uses the same rain admission, result validation, absorption, heat and reaction code. Its callback receives `{ phase, fields, headOffsets, dt, flow, tiltX, tiltZ, motion }`, with `phase` equal to `aqueous` or `oil`, and resolves `{ fields, flux, outflowM3 }`. `liquids.transport(request)` uploads that input and encodes compute and readback copies in one GPU submission. It rejects overlapping transport requests and borrows the supplied device without acquiring or destroying another one.

`rain` in world options uses mm/h; the low-level GPU and renderer parameters use m/s. `rainDomain` selects one topology domain; its default `null` retains rain over all exposed cells for existing callers. `stepAsync()` prepares rain once and sends no additional GPU rain. The older `prepareWater()` / `upload()` / `step()` / `readWaterResult()` sequence remains available for callers that supply a precomputed aqueous result to synchronous `world.step()`. Upload the prepared fields and `world.waterHeadOffsets`, and keep GPU rain at zero in that path. Both phase results must match their exact prepared donor volumes and gather results.

Await `stepAsync()` before calling `step()`, another `stepAsync()`, `applyBrush()`, `snapshot()` or `restore()` on the same world; those methods reject while an asynchronous step is pending. A callback must treat supplied arrays as borrowed input. Copy them before transferring their buffers across a worker boundary because the owner still needs them for validation and the next phase. If the oil callback fails, the aqueous phase may already have committed. The failed tick does not advance the material clock; stop and rebuild or restore a valid checkpoint instead of retrying that tick against partially advanced state. Surface Lab handles this through its error-stop state.

For a worker-owned world, use the asynchronous facade:

```javascript

    createSurfaceFieldTopology, loadSurfaceFieldMaterials,
    createSurfaceFieldGpuRuntime, createSurfaceFieldWorker,
} from '/engine/sim/surfaceFields/index.js';

const topology = createSurfaceFieldTopology({ n: 16, seams: true });

    topology, materials, runoff: true, transport: request => liquids.transport(request),

await world.step(0.05, { rain: 30, tiltX: 0.05 });
const checkpoint = await world.snapshot();
await world.restore(checkpoint);
world.dispose();
liquids.dispose();
```

The facade serializes `step()`, `applyBrush()`, `snapshot()` and `restore()` requests. Its `fields`, `auxiliary`, `emissions`, `flowSources`, `liquidProperties`, `runoff`, `steps` and `timeSeconds` describe the latest completed response. `stats()` reads that completed mirror synchronously. These arrays are presentation copies, not writable material authority; edits go through `applyBrush()` or `restore()`. The facade's `chemicals.cell(index)` exposes only carbonate mass, initial carbonate mass, HCl fraction and aqueous temperature needed by presentation and the structural bridge. It is not the full synchronous chemical-owner API. In default borrowed mode, `dispose()` terminates the worker and rejects pending work. Await outstanding steps before releasing the separate borrowed-device GPU runtime.

For suspension, create the world with `supports: true`, create `SurfaceFieldStructure` with `{ suspended: true }`, then call `configureSupports(definitions)` before the first material step. A definition contains `{ id, domain, cellIndex, anchor, attachment, material, radiusM, breakingForceN }`; positions use world-space metres. The worker facade serializes `configureSupports()` and `applySupportBrush({ id, segment, tool, massKg, temperatureK })` alongside other edits. The synchronous world exposes the same operations directly. `tool: 'ignite'` supplies heat rather than adding a mass dose. `supportFrame`, `supportLoads` and `structuralState` describe the latest completed response.

Material ownership, native motion and presentation therefore have an explicit ordered handoff rather than independent clocks.

## Checkpoints and limits

`encodeSurfaceFieldCheckpoint()` defaults to exact Float32 field runs and retained pine/Matter owner state, protected by CRC32. The outer surface schema remains version 1 and declares `liquidModelVersion: 2`. The chemical owner uses version 4 when support chemistry is enabled, version 3 when only runoff is enabled or retained, and version 2 for existing direct-routing callers. Version 3 saves the parcel configuration, IDs, trajectories, finite species inventories and heat. Version 4 adds the support chemistry profile, namespace and exchange/reaction ledgers. Legacy version 1/2 restores start with no airborne parcels and preserve the receiving world's configured runoff mode. Restore validates topology identity, material identity, geometry, configuration, reaction profile hash, and agreement between native owners and derived fields before committing. Restoring a valid chemical checkpoint preserves the next step, including both phase energies and transported composition.

Support-enabled world saves also contain `supportsConfigured`, `supports` and the optional completed `motion` frame. The v4 configuration marker must agree with the presence of the support owner. The support snapshot retains every fiber/steel segment, liquid phase, cut, geometry, clock and reaction ledger. World restore reconciles opposite signed mass, water and heat exchanges between panel and support chemistry. Dropping one owner from an exchanged inventory rejects instead of silently removing matter. Legacy chemical checkpoints migrate into the configured support-capable inventory without inventing support material.

Runoff restore requires explicit `enabled` and `capacity` fields and reconciles active and deposited parcel counts with the next identity. Missing configuration or inconsistent counters reject before replacing the live owner. Disabled configurations can retain a completed history with no active parcels.

Oil and aqueous energies are retained separately in Float64 arrays so a trace water film does not lose its heat beside a large oil inventory. Earlier version 2 checkpoints without the additive `aqueousEnergyJ` array recover the available aqueous heat from their total and oil energies once; subsequent saves retain both arrays explicitly. Restore still checks their sum against each Matter owner's retained energy.

The playground drains both pending owners, awaits the worker's exact snapshot, and saves `snapshot.structure`, `snapshot.supportGeometry` and its lab settings. Loading stages replacement material, native and strand owners before swapping the live scene. Native poses, velocities, masses, support state and damage replay come from the existing Blast/PhysX checkpoint contract. PhysX reconstructs actors and constraints; it does not retain solver warm-start/contact caches. Material checkpoint continuation at the same supplied geometry is exact, while future native trajectories are not promised to be bit-identical. Native Flow visualization restarts after a load. A legacy checkpoint without structural metadata rebuilds its native sheet and reports that motion was not restored. Loaded files use manual experiment triggers, so the demo's scheduled sequence cannot inject a new dose unexpectedly. Earlier three-material checkpoints with no chemical owner remain admissible only for topologies without calcite; their new chemical owner is empty and their native fields remain exact.

An explicit nonzero `tolerance` creates a bounded approximate preview. Decoding that preview requires `allowApproximate: true`. The retained world rejects it as authoritative state. The playground's preview inspector reports byte counts and field error while leaving the live state untouched.

Earlier version 1 liquid checkpoints migrate their acid carrier water into the shared aqueous owner and import the separate free-water field once. Old checkpoints did not store aqueous energy; migration initializes that previously unavailable quantity at the declared temperature reference and records it. Resaving writes the current configured chemical version, so subsequent restores do not re-add free water. The old solid cooling export remains a legacy ledger term. Removing the liquid-model marker from a new chemical owner rejects the checkpoint instead of triggering migration.

The water model caps each donor's outgoing volume before gathering inflow across unequal areas. Both liquid phases retain material-chart cell identities after fragments move; completed native poses and surviving links update pressure heights and disconnect old paths. This does not create liquid bridges when unrelated pieces meet or simulate free-surface sloshing. Pine uses prescribed atmospheric oxygen and its existing one-dimensional layered solid model. Oil supplies stoichiometric oxygen from an explicit atmospheric reservoir. Native Flow solves the derived gas field, while optical smoke concentrations do not assert soot chemistry or exact gas inventory transfer. The installed addon does not expose the newer finite scalar-deposit or gas-to-wood feedback contracts, so those couplings remain unavailable. The support helper's authored local heat return is separate from native gas feedback. Copper properties remain constant. Blast collision boxes retain authored geometry until a leaf is fully consumed; partial etching does not reconstruct native shapes. Calcite heat capacity, conductivity and mechanical stress calibration remain unavailable rather than being fabricated.

(Sources: `engine/sim/combustion/WoodCombustion.js`, `SurfaceFieldSupports.js` and `SurfaceFieldWorld.js`.)
